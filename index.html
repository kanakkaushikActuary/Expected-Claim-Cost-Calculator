CS1 — GLM Foundations

        
Exponential dispersion family. Both the Poisson (frequency) and Gamma (severity) distributions belong to the exponential dispersion family, so a single GLM framework applies across both models.

        
Link function. A log-link is used for both models: g(μ) = log(μ) = Xβ. It is the canonical link for Poisson, keeps predictions strictly positive, and gives a multiplicative rating structure — the industry-standard tariff form.

        
Offset. Exposure enters the frequency models as log(Exposure) with a fixed coefficient of 1 (an offset), converting the model from predicting raw counts to predicting a rate per unit exposure.

        
Mean-variance relationship. Poisson assumes Var(Y) = μ (count data); Gamma assumes Var(Y) = φμ² (constant coefficient of variation) — appropriate for strictly positive, right-skewed claim amounts.

        
Deviance & dispersion. Model adequacy is assessed via scaled deviance and the estimated dispersion parameter, shown on the Model Diagnostics tab.

      

      

        
CS2 — Pricing Application

        
Frequency-severity decomposition. Pure premium is built as E[Cost] = E[Frequency] × E[Severity], the standard two-part pricing model separating "how often" from "how much".

        
Peril segmentation. Minor and major incidents are modelled as separate GLMs rather than one blended model, reflecting real pricing practice since rating factors often act differently on frequency vs severity for small vs large claims.

        
Rating factors. Driver age, vehicle age/type, region, NCD and prior-claims history are classic tariff rating factors; their fitted GLM coefficients are the relativities multiplied together to build the technical price.

        
Technical vs office premium. This app returns the technical (risk) premium only. A full office premium would add expense loadings, profit margin, reinsurance cost, and commission.

      

    

    

      
Suggested extensions (for a competition/portfolio submission)

      
    
        
  • Replace the separate frequency×severity GLMs with a single Tweedie compound Poisson-Gamma GLM (power parameter 1<p<2) to model pure premium directly.
    • 
        
  • Add a large-loss threshold and model excess losses with a Generalised Pareto Distribution (extreme value theory, CS2).
    • 
        
  • Introduce Bühlmann–Straub credibility weighting to blend individual experience with the GLM base rate.
    • 
        
  • Add a GAM / GBM benchmark to test for non-linear rating factor effects the GLM's linear predictor misses.
    • 
        
  • Incorporate an IBNR / claims development layer so the calculator prices for ultimate, not just reported, cost.
